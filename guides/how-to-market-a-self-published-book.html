Author marketing guide

How to market a self-published book

Book marketing is not one launch-day announcement. It is a system that helps the right reader recognize the book, trust the offer, and find a clear way to buy or recommend it.

By Christine Leninger · Reviewed August 3, 2026

Begin with positioning, not promotion

Write one sentence that identifies the primary reader, what the book offers, and why this book is a relevant choice. That sentence should guide the subtitle, description, categories, author bio, website copy, outreach list, and content themes. A book for everyone is difficult to introduce to anyone.

Study comparable books to understand reader expectations—not to copy their language. Note covers, descriptions, reviews, price points, categories, and the conversations those authors are already part of.

Make the retail page conversion-ready

Clear first impression

The cover must remain legible as a thumbnail. The title and subtitle should set accurate expectations. The opening lines of the description should earn the next scroll.

Complete book information

Use accurate categories, keywords, formats, contributor names, author bio, and pricing. Check every live edition and retailer link from a reader’s perspective.

Build assets you control

Retail algorithms and social platforms can change. An author website and permission-based email list give readers a stable home. Create a focused book page with the promise, description, formats, purchase links, author credibility, and a useful next step.

Choose one or two recurring content themes that connect the book to real reader questions. Reuse a strong idea across an email, article, short video, podcast pitch, or event topic instead of inventing unrelated daily posts.

A workable launch sequence

  1. Eight to twelve weeks out: finalize positioning, audience list, book page, advance-copy plan, and outreach targets.
  2. Four to eight weeks out: distribute advance copies where appropriate, prepare content and email, confirm events or interviews, and test every link.
  3. Launch week: coordinate email, social, partners, appearances, and purchase links around a simple reader action.
  4. After launch: follow up, share useful excerpts and conversations, improve the retail page, and keep pitching relevant opportunities.

Handle reviews ethically

Invite honest feedback from relevant readers; do not require a positive rating. Disclose material relationships, avoid review swaps designed to manipulate ratings, and follow the current rules of each retailer and review platform. A review service should support legitimate outreach—not sell guaranteed praise.

Use patterns in reader feedback to improve the description, FAQ, outreach, and future content. Do not edit the book’s promise around a single reaction.

Measure what each activity is meant to do

GoalUseful signalQuestion
AwarenessQualified reach, visits, invitationsAre the right people seeing it?
InterestBook-page engagement, email signupsDoes the promise earn attention?
ActionPurchase-link clicks, sales where availableIs the next step clear and credible?
AdvocacyVoluntary reviews, referrals, invitationsAre readers recommending it?

Use tagged links where possible and review results over a meaningful period. No ethical marketer can guarantee bestseller status, a specific number of reviews, or a fixed sales result.

Your next three moves

  1. 1. Write the one-sentence reader promise.
  2. 2. Audit the retail page and every purchase link.
  3. 3. Choose one audience channel and one measurable action for the next 30 days.
