Independent publishing guide

How to self-publish a book in nine connected steps

Self-publishing gives you control over the book and the decisions. It also makes you the publisher. The sequence below helps you avoid paying for design, files, or promotion before the foundation is ready.

By Christine Leninger · Reviewed August 3, 2026

  1. 01

    Define the reader and the job of the book

    Name the primary reader, the problem or experience that brings them to the book, and what should change after reading. For nonfiction, this sharpens the promise. For memoir and fiction, it helps clarify positioning, genre expectations, and comparable titles.

  2. 02

    Finish the right manuscript—not just a draft

    Complete the manuscript, then evaluate structure before polishing sentences. A manuscript review or developmental edit can identify gaps, repetition, pacing, and audience misalignment. Resolve major changes before copyediting and proofreading.

  3. 03

    Create an editorial sequence

    Developmental work, line or copy editing, and proofreading solve different problems. Not every book needs the same level of each, but proofreading is most useful after the text and layout are stable. Keep a clean master file and approve changes deliberately.

  4. 04

    Choose formats, trim, and design

    Decide whether the book needs paperback, hardcover, ebook, or another format. A cover must work as a retail thumbnail and meet the printer’s full-wrap specifications. The interior must be readable and built for the chosen trim size; print and ebook files usually need separate preparation.

  5. 05

    Choose the ISBN and imprint approach

    Each format and edition may require its own identifier. A platform-provided ISBN can reduce upfront cost but may limit the imprint name and where the identifier can be used. An ISBN you purchase gives you greater control. Make this decision before finalizing cover files.

  6. 06

    Prepare metadata and retail positioning

    Write the book description for the intended reader. Choose accurate categories and keywords, set an informed list price, and make the author bio and contributors consistent. Metadata should describe the book honestly; keyword stuffing weakens clarity.

  7. 07

    Upload, preview, and order a physical proof

    Use the platform previewer, but do not stop there. Review a printed proof for margins, page breaks, images, spine alignment, color, readability, front matter, and links or references. Correct the source files and repeat if material changes remain.

  8. 08

    Build the launch before publication day

    Prepare the retail page, author site or landing page, email sequence, advance-reader process, social assets, and outreach calendar. Reviews must be voluntary and comply with each retailer’s rules. A launch date is a coordination point—not the end of marketing.

  9. 09

    Measure and maintain the book

    Check retail listings, links, author profiles, reader questions, and the promotion activities you can actually measure. Keep source files and edition records organized. Update the plan from evidence instead of changing the book’s positioning every week.

The most expensive mistake is doing steps out of order

Cover design before positioning, layout before editing, or advertising before the retail page is ready can create avoidable rework. A short planning phase protects the later investment.
